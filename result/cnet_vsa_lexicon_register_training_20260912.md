# Lexicon v3.1: register-diverse training with contrast pairs, evaluated on untouched paraphrase families (2026-09-12)

Gate: `make vsa_routing_arena` (rows `lex_full`, `lex_trained`; frozen_model.json re-pinned for v3.1).
Regression set: `questions_fresh_20260912.tsv` (88, written after the v3 freeze, never trained on).

## Why

The post-freeze check showed the v3 trained table's lead was bound to the teacher's question register:
parity with Qwen3-Embedding-4B on confusable siblings phrased in domain terms, a 25-point loss on
colloquial phrasing, where training on teacher questions alone had made the table worse than untrained.
Adding more teacher-register questions would push colloquial top-1 down again.

## What changed

1. **Training families in mixed registers** (`questions_train_v2.tsv`), all generated by the local teacher
   (port 8081) from training sources only:
   - the 5,326 v3 teacher questions, kept;
   - colloquial paraphrases of 3 of them per corpus, two registers each: a casual search-box question and a
     plain-words description of the situation;
   - everyday descriptions of 2 corpus sentences per corpus (the sentences the trainer already pairs);
   - **sibling contrast pairs**: for 400 confusable capsule pairs (from the v3 confusion lists), two casual
     questions that belong to one side and not the other, both directions, with the sibling recorded as an
     explicit negative;
   - **polysemy contrast pairs**: for everyday words that appear in unrelated capsules (shell, function,
     gear, cell, table, thread, bridge, ...), one casual question per sense, each with the other capsule as
     the explicit negative.
   The corpus sentences (16 per corpus, technical register) stay in training as before.
2. **Trainer**: an optional third column names an explicit negative capsule; when that capsule violates the
   margin it is the negative for the update, otherwise the hardest other centroid is (as before).
3. **Family separation.** Every generated line carries a family id (its source question, sentence, pair
   or word). Evaluation families are disjoint from training families by construction:
   - `questions_colloquial_eval.tsv`: paraphrases of the FIRST frozen evaluation question per corpus
     (sources never in training);
   - `questions_contrast_eval.tsv`: contrast questions for a held-out 30% of confusable pairs (which includes
     the 24 pairs of the regression set) and a held-out 30% of polysemous words.
   Near-duplicates of frozen evaluation questions are dropped from every set.
4. **Lenient labels, same for both systems** (`alternates.tsv`): a capsule may have justified alternate
   targets. Candidates are corpora that are mutual top-3 centroid neighbours under BOTH the untrained CNET
   lexicon and Qwen3-4B (so neither system's geometry alone chooses them); each directed candidate is
   judged by the local teacher from three sentences of each ("would collection B correctly and fully answer
   a question meant for A?"), and only YES pairs are kept. Every scorer reports strict top-1 and lenient
   top-1 (top-1 in {gold} + alternates) with the same file; top-3 stays as the loose bound.

Readings.
- **Mixing registers did what the note predicted.** Colloquial top-1 rose 19 points on the untouched
  colloquial families (37.5 -> 56.4) and 10 on the regression set's colloquial half, while the frozen
  teacher-register questions ALSO improved (60.0 -> 63.0) and held-out sentences and separability did not
  move down. Nothing was traded.
- **Against the 4B model** the trained table now leads on the regression set (52.3 vs 47.7), on the
  untouched colloquial families (56.4 vs 49.4 top-1; top-3 73.8 vs 72.5), on the frozen questions, and on
  held-out polysemous words. It is at parity on held-out sibling-pair top-1 (31.0 vs 32.1, inside the
  tolerance) and still behind on the regression set's colloquial half (37.5 vs 50.0), on contrast top-3
  (51.9 vs 67.9), and on held-out sentence ranking (34.5 vs 37.6). Those are recorded as losses.
- **Training against confusable capsules generalised to held-out pairs**: on pairs never trained on, the
  table's misses go to the sibling 34 times against 151 for the transformer, and the regression set's
  sibling half went from 43.8 to 66.7. What the table still lacks is the near miss: when it is wrong on a
  colloquial contrast question it is wrong by a lot (top-2 45.9 vs 62.2), the bag-of-words failure on
  phrasing it has not seen, not sibling confusion.
- Lenient labels changed no verdict: every system gains about one point.

## v3.2: vocabulary coverage of the everyday register (same day, after the sweep above)

The trainer can only move words that have entries. Under the v3.1 table 60.5% of the colloquial
evaluation questions and 62.5% of the contrast questions contained at least one word with no learned
vector (teacher questions: 22.5%), and top-1 fell with every unknown word (contrast: 39.3% with none,
30.6% with one, 19.0% with two or more). Fix: the lex_full BUILD reads the training-register questions
as extra lines of their corpus (`--lexicon-extra`, a second build directory), so everyday words that
recur get Random-Indexing vectors and phrases; the RI and distilled rows and the trainer's sentence pairs
stay on the pure corpora. Evaluation text never enters the build.

tolerance and recorded.

## Independent post-freeze set: a different writer, held-out content, everyday register

No human set was available, so the closest substitute was built: after the v3.2 freeze,
Mistral-Small-3.2-24B (a different model family from the Ternary-Bonsai-27B that wrote every training
paraphrase, and the same model that wrote the frozen teacher questions) wrote 773 questions from
HELD-OUT sources only: 600 everyday questions from the two held-out sentences of sampled corpora (A),
120 for held-out confusable pairs, one per side (B), 53 for held-out polysemous words (C). The same
model then judged per-question alternates from the gold capsule's near neighbours (349 questions got at
least one), applied to every system. `questions_indep_mistral_20260912.tsv`, pinned; never trained on.

| system | all 773 strict / lenient / top-3 | A everyday (600) strict / lenient | B siblings (120) | C polysemy (53) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| stem | 11.5 / 14.8 / 22.2 | 12.0 / 15.5 | 5.0 / 5.8 | 20.8 / 26.4 |
| lex_full (untrained) | 18.0 / 22.6 / 31.1 | 18.7 / 24.0 | 9.2 / 10.0 | 30.2 / 35.8 |
| lex_trained (v3.2) | 22.1 / 26.3 / 35.2 | 23.5 / 27.2 | 10.8 / 15.0 | 32.1 / 41.5 |
| Qwen3-Embedding-4B | **27.6 / 34.4 / 44.0** | **28.7 / 35.3** | **18.3 / 24.2** | **35.8 / 47.2** |

Readings.
- **The transformer leads on every section**: 5.5 points strict, 8 lenient, 9 on top-3. Training still
  helps (18.0 -> 22.1 over the untrained table) but far less than on the sets written in the training
  paraphraser's register (+23 there). So part of the earlier colloquial lead was the generator's idiom
  learned back: the "everyday register" the table learned is Bonsai's everyday register, and Mistral's
  is another one.
- **The set is hard for both.** Questions written from a corpus's two held-out tail sentences ask about
  content the centroid barely covers; even the transformer gets 28.7% on the everyday section, and both
  systems route the tail sentences themselves at only 34 to 38%. Two things changed at once, register
  and source content; the mid-corpus control below separates them.
- **Writer family alone is not the explanation.** The same Mistral wrote the frozen teacher-register
  questions, where the table leads by 8 points. The lead depends on register and content position, not
  on which model holds the pen.

**Control: same writer, same register, in-corpus content.** Mistral wrote 400 more everyday questions from
train sentences at positions no training pair or paraphrase ever used (`questions_indep_mistral_mid_20260912.tsv`,
pinned, never trained on), so only the source content changed.

| system | strict top-1 | lenient top-1 | top-3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| lex_full (untrained) | 26.5 | 32.0 | 42.5 |
| lex_trained (v3.2) | 24.8 | 31.0 | 41.5 |
| Qwen3-Embedding-4B | **37.5** | **44.8** | **57.5** |

The control settles it. With in-corpus content the transformer gains 9 points over the tail-content set
(28.7 -> 37.5) while the table gains one (23.5 -> 24.8), and training gives the table nothing here
(24.8 vs 26.5 untrained, inside noise). The gap in Mistral's everyday register is 12 to 14 points
regardless of where the content sits. So the factor is the register, and the colloquial lead measured
earlier (+7.7 on the Bonsai-written families, +8 on the regression set) is largely the training
generator's idiom learned back, not a general command of everyday phrasing.

**Verdict on the specialization thesis, as measured.** A task-trained static table beats the general
embedding model on the distribution it was trained toward: the teacher's question register (+8) and
the training paraphraser's everyday register (+7.7 on untouched families). It loses by 12 to 14 points
on an everyday register from a writer it never saw, by 5 to 9 on held-out content from that writer, and
by 4 on held-out sentence ranking. The advantage is real and bounded by register coverage; every
register the table has to serve has to be in its training families, from more than one writer. The
ceiling underneath is composition: with a vector for every word, unfamiliar phrasing still reads as a
bag. The next data step was pursued (cross-writer experiment below) and did not help. The human-written
set remains the reference.

## Cross-writer experiment: does a second writer's register transfer to a third?

Two more writers wrote everyday-register training families from training sources only (casual rewrites
of the 5 teacher training questions per corpus, everyday questions from 4 unused corpus sentences per
corpus): Mistral-Small-3.2-24B and gemma4 (thinking off). Each also wrote a 600-question evaluation set
from the two held-out sentences of the same sampled corpora, with per-question alternates. Three training
variants: v2 (Bonsai only), +Mistral, +gemma, +both. Each writer's evaluation set is unseen for the variant
that excludes it. All sets pinned in manifest.json; no evaluation text in any training set.

| training | frozen q top-1 | regression 88 | Mistral everyday (600) strict / lenient | gemma everyday (600) strict / lenient | Mistral in-corpus control (400) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| v2 (Bonsai) | 64.2 | 55.7 | 17.8 / 23.7 | 24.2 / 30.3 | 24.8 / 31.0 |
| + Mistral | 63.4 | 53.4 | 18.8 / 25.3 (seen) | 27.5 / 33.5 | 26.0 / 33.2 (seen) |
| + gemma | 62.6 | 56.8 | 19.0 / 24.7 | 25.0 / 31.3 (seen) | 25.8 / 32.0 |
| + both | 61.9 | 51.1 | 18.8 / 24.0 (seen) | 25.7 / 31.8 (seen) | 26.8 / 34.2 (seen) |
| untrained lex_full | 49.1 | 35.2 | 16.7 / 21.8 | 20.2 / 26.5 | 26.5 / 32.0 |
| Qwen3-Embedding-4B | 56.0 | 47.7 | **24.2 / 33.0** | **30.0 / 38.7** | **37.5 / 44.8** |

Readings.
- **Register does not transfer, and barely even sticks.** Training on a writer's own everyday register
  moves that writer's held-out-content set by about one point (17.8 -> 18.8, 24.2 -> 25.0). The unseen
  writer moves by one to three. Training on both writers costs four points on the regression set and
  two on the frozen questions, for no gain anywhere that was not trained on.
- **Why the earlier colloquial gain was not register.** Lexical overlap between each evaluation question
  and the closest training pair of its own capsule (max Jaccard on content words): the Bonsai colloquial
  families, where training gained 19 points, have median 0.18 and 16% of questions share at least 30% of
  their content words with a training pair; the regression set 0.17 / 14%; the three held-out-content sets
  0.10 to 0.11 / 2 to 3%. The colloquial families were paraphrases of questions written from the very
  sentences the trainer pairs with. What the table learned was the content of its pairs, in any register;
  what it did not learn was the corpus.
- **So the specialization is content-bound, not register-bound.** A trained static table routes well
  the content it has pairs for, in the registers those pairs were written in, and its advantage over the
  general embedding model ends at the edge of that content: 5 to 6 points behind on everyday questions
  about held-out sentences, 11 behind on everyday questions about in-corpus sentences it had no pair for,
  4 behind on the sentences themselves. The transformer generalises over content; the bag of vectors
  does not.
- **The data lever is exhausted.** More writers and more registers will not close this. The remaining
  levers are more pairs per corpus (which is memorising more of the corpus, with a table that does not
  grow) or a composition model at query time, which is a different runtime cost and a different project.

Decision: the shipped model stays v3.2 (trained on questions_train_v2.tsv). The three unseen-writer sets
are reported by the gate with recorded-loss claims (Qwen3-4B >= trained lexicon on each). Footprint of the
largest variant, for the record: 42.0 MB table (11,796 words, 8,192 phrases, 10,237 subwords), 6.1 us per
query, 10.8 s build, 445 s to train 52k pairs. Nothing at runtime changed by more than a few percent; the
whole cost of this experiment was offline generation (about 4 GPU-hours on the local writers).

## Gate

`make vsa_routing_arena` now builds lex_full over the corpora plus the training-register questions
(v3.2), trains on questions_train_v2.tsv, scores the three post-freeze sets with
alternates for every row and for the transformer caches, and PASSES with: 48 frozen values, claims
(new: trained >= Qwen3-4B on the regression set and on the colloquial evaluation set; recorded loss on
contrast top-3), and one-sided regression floors for `lex_trained` on the three sets (a value may rise,
never fall below its recorded level). The regression set is never trained on.

WITHHELD: generated-text quality; QA answer correctness; open-ended language. The evaluation questions of
every set are model-written (local teacher, assistant); the human-written check at `questions_human.tsv`
is still the one that matters most.
