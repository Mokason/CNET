## Why the legacy stream is excluded

Author-separated Astra source review, reconciled against the checked-out code:

- `tools/cnetd.c` ordinary log tail is gated on misses and misses early returns.
  Successes therefore are not a complete demand denominator; timestamps are
  recorded after processing, not at decision-time ingress.
- `tools/roe_front_door.c` benchmark calls the same miss writer without a test
  origin. `source` describes answer production, not human/test/agent origin.
- `src/cnet_live_miss.c` typed writer omits time and creates repeated
  `tm_<domain>_<input>` identifiers. Its `certified=1` simply reflects `has_out`.
  `src/cnet_rlm.c` can feed CNET's own successful outputs into that writer. Such
  flags cannot establish independent targets for this experiment.
- Existing query escaping and unchecked/unlocked writers expose malformed or
  interleaved records. Current source shows a possible malformed-record mechanism;
  no exact historical deployed-writer attribution is claimed for the three lines.
- `PEER` is caller-supplied informational text. A private socket shared by one OS
  account does not separate its human, agent and test clients. Adding an arbitrary
  origin field would not resolve provenance.

These findings concern suitability for the new experiment. They do not establish
that every historical row was generated by CNET or that every existing learner
consumed such rows. No live legacy learning path was altered by this work.
