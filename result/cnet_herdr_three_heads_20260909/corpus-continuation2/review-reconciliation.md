# Contract-only review reconciliation

The first review independently derived all 128 labels with author labels
withheld. All statuses, operations and original-input integers matched. That
agreement is label evidence, not a CNET score. Five substantive-review findings
and nine sample/reporting observations were separately examined below.

The first packet retained author family names, which can hint at categories.
The second review therefore covers the entire revised population using only
opaque IDs and texts: family names, status, operation and key are all withheld.
The stronger final derivation does not rely on an unchanged-row inheritance
from the first, less fully masked packet. Both requests and responses remain
preserved, and neither is a CNET scoring run.

| 5 | Contract misread (`n2_23`, `n2_30`). Selecting an operand and applying its single requested conversion does not create a second task. The shared contract explicitly permits natural descriptions of one conversion. No row changed. |

Only contract and draft requests informed these decisions. No candidate code,
scores, scorer outputs or previous failure examples were inspected. The
coordinator's source-freeze notification contained metadata only and did not
change the author contract, selection rules, quotas or repair requests.

The second review returned all 128 final labels with no mismatch and three
findings. Each is reconciled in `review-cycle-2-dispositions.json`; all rely
on interpretations excluded by the explicit shared contract. Five nonblocking
limitations are also preserved there. The stop condition is resolved findings,
not absence of reviewer criticism. Mechanical checks verify complete, unique
review coverage and exactly the four intended changes since the first review.
`admission.json` records the frozen population and unchanged floors; neither
review is counted as CNET accuracy or native execution evidence.
